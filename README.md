## The idea

Most AI advice is either hype or homework — breathless threads, or a link to a two-hour webinar nobody finishes. These playbooks are neither. Each one is a short, specific guide to adopting AI in a particular setting, written from having actually done it, and stripped of the noise.

They're deliberately **not** an awesome-list of links. The authored methodology leads; curated resources sit underneath it, never in front.

## The spine: two things every playbook shares

**1. The six-stage model.** Every playbook uses the same diagnosis of *where a person or team actually is* — sorted by internal experience, not tools deployed:

> Nomad → Observer → Dabbler → Integrator → **Architect** → Innovator

The full model is its own canonical repo: **[ai-adoption-model](https://github.com/VictorOsondu/ai-adoption-model)**.

**2. The ICE Method.** How to actually spend your AI effort, three ways:

- **Improve** — do work you already do, faster and better.
- **Create** — make things you couldn't make before.
- **Educate** — build the understanding to use AI well and know when not to.

## The house standard

These aren't awesome-lists, so they follow their own standard instead of a borrowed one. Every playbook in the series has the same shape and the same README structure — that consistency is what makes seven repos read as one deliberate body of work. See **[PLAYBOOK-STANDARD.md](PLAYBOOK-STANDARD.md)**.

## Why "field-tested" matters here

Anyone can collect links. Far fewer people have stood in a room and rolled AI training out to staff who didn't ask for it. Where a playbook is marked *field-tested*, it means the methodology was used with a real organisation — and the playbook says which. Where it isn't yet, it says so plainly, and marks the material as adapted rather than delivered. No invented provenance.
